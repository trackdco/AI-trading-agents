VIDEO 3: "AMT vs ICT" (66sow9MjlSM)

ICT-TO-AMT MAPPING (the translation table he draws, assembled from his statements):
- Fair value gap → buying/selling imbalance: "a fair value gap is just like a, you know, a buying imbalance or a selling imbalance. So, it just means there's an indifference between buyers and sellers at that area... the market can come back to rebalance that gap. It's not enough to form a trade off of alone" [00:30]. AMT translation: an FVG is just an imbalanced (single-print-like) zone the auction may rotate back through; direction of the rotation is set by value/condition, not by the gap.
- Order blocks / liquidity sweeps / FVGs collectively → "they're just inefficiencies or, you know, nuanced things in the market" [00:30]; usable only inside a condition read.
- Break of structure (BOS) → NOT a condition change: "that's a break of structure... that would be bearish based on swings. So, that doesn't give you a read on market condition, though" [02:31]–[03:03]. AMT condition change = break from balance / acceptance-rejection at value edges, not swing labels.
- ICT "equilibrium" (50% of the dealing range) → the middle of the auction, i.e., a no-trade zone: shorting an FVG with equilibrium confluence "didn't work, right? That's at the edge of value, that didn't work" [05:37]–[06:07]; and longing an FVG mid-range: "You're buying in the middle of value. This is not really a trade location majority of the time. It's in the middle of the auction. You want to trade at the edges" [07:39].
- Equal highs + FVG short (classic ICT) → invalidated by acceptance: "if you took a short here, that wouldn't work because we saw acceptance, so we expected it to go to here... acceptance inside of value, we expect it to run to the other side" [08:41].
- Liquidity sweep → in his language, a deviation of a value edge that either finds acceptance back inside (trade the traversal) or rejects (condition intact). (Implicit throughout [09:43]–[10:14].)
- His overall verdict: "basing your... whole framework off of inefficiencies in the market. It's just simply not enough to have a repeatable process over time... You can't get a read on what the market condition is" [11:16]–[11:47].

HIS MODEL OF WHY STRATEGIES FAIL:
- Setups/models are not frameworks: "it's the difference between a framework... and a trading setup or a model or a strategy. They're two different things" [00:00]–[00:30]. Signal-tools "don't give you any read on what market condition actually is" [00:30].
