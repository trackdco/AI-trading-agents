# Evidence Survey: Strategy Classes for Intraday NQ (London Window 03:00–06:00 ET + NY Morning)

**Scope note.** Prepared for a small systematic NQ shop; 1-min OHLCV 2023–2026, order-book depth/CVD June 2025+. Every section states: claimed edge, economic mechanism (who pays), evidence strength, **explicit decay status**, and fit to our instrument/window/data. A recurring meta-finding: the two best-documented effects in *our* window (overnight drift, around-the-clock return concentration) both have **published, dated decay evidence**, and the single most on-point negative study in the literature is on **MNQ itself**. Priors should be calibrated accordingly.

**Cross-cutting negative result (read first).** Mesfin, ["Structural Limits of OHLCV-Based Intraday Signals in MNQ Futures: A Systematic Falsification Study"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04004) (arXiv 2605.04004, May 2026): 14 signal families (momentum, breakout, mean-reversion, volume-based) on 947 days of 5-min MNQ 2021–2025, walk-forward, t≥2.0, ≥30 trades, 2-point round-trip friction. **Zero survivors**; max gross edge 0.07–1.50 pts/trade, below friction. Implication: on our exact instrument, *naive* OHLCV signals at 5-min granularity are dead after costs. Anything we test must be (a) conditioned on structural flow/timing information the falsification grid didn't span (session clocks, closing imbalances, scheduled announcements, book depth), or (b) low-frequency enough per trade that a 1–2 pt cost hurdle is small relative to expected move.

## 1. Intraday momentum (Gao–Han–Li–Zhou first-half-hour → last-half-hour)

**Claim.** First half-hour return (measured from prior close, i.e., including overnight) positively predicts last half-hour return. SPY 1993–2013: scaled slope 6.94, R² 1.6% (2.6% adding the 12th half-hour), significant in- and out-of-sample; stronger on high-volatility, high-volume, recession, and macro-news days. Published JFE 2018 — top-3 journal, high evidential grade.

**Mechanism / who pays.** Two documented channels. (i) Late-informed trading and infrequent institutional rebalancing (Gao et al.). (ii) The sharper identification: **short-gamma hedging flow** — [Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens, "Hedging Demand and Market Intraday Momentum," JFE 2021](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3760365): option market makers and leveraged-ETF issuers who are short gamma must trade *with* the day's move into the close; rest-of-day return predicts last-30-min return across **60+ futures, 1974–2020**, and the effect **reverts over subsequent days** (price pressure, not information). The payers are hedgers buying rebalancing certainty — a structurally recurring, non-informational counterparty, which is the best kind.

**Replication.** [Li, Sakkas & Urquhart, "Intraday Time Series Momentum: Global Evidence," J. Financial Markets 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S138641812100001X): significant in ~12–16 of 16 developed markets in- and out-of-sample; stronger under low liquidity/high vol. Practitioner extension: [Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon, "Beat the Market" (SSRN 4824172, 2024)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4824172) — all-day "noise-area" breakout version on SPY, 2007–early-2024, 19.6% ann., Sharpe 1.33 net (claimed). [Quantitativo replicated on **ES and NQ futures**](https://www.quantitativo.com/p/intraday-momentum-for-es-and-nq): NQ 24.3% ann. vs 17.6% B&H, Sharpe 1.67, +6 bps/trade, 38% win rate — but only after changing the lookback 14d→90d (parameter sensitivity flag).

**Decay status: NOT cleanly decayed — regime-dependent, with the sign of the regime *favorable* recently.** Notable nuance from replications: the classic signal was roughly **flat 2010–2017 and revived from 2018 onward** (consistent with growth of leveraged-ETF AUM and short-dated options/0DTE hedging demand). Contrary evidence exists: a [Swedish-market thesis (2024)](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1878991/FULLTEXT01.pdf) finds weak predictability in small markets, and at least one replication attributes part of the original result to data mining. Related caution: [Baltussen, Da & Soebhag, "End-of-Day Reversal" (2024)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5039009) shows the *cross-section* of stocks reverses in the last 30 min even as the *index* exhibits momentum — don't port index logic to single names or vice versa.

**Fit for us.** The canonical expression needs the 15:30–16:00 ET close — outside both our windows. What transfers: the *mechanism template* (find scheduled hedging/rebalancing flow near a session boundary) and the noise-area breakout formulation, which is testable at the 03:00 London open and 09:30 NY open. Mesfin's falsification is the counterweight — his grid included momentum/breakout families on MNQ and found nothing net of costs; the Concretum/Quantitativo results are single-group, non-peer-reviewed, and parameter-sensitive.

**Sources:** [Gao et al. JFE 2018 (SSRN)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2440866) · [JFE version](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X18301351) · [Baltussen et al. JFE 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X21001598) · [Li–Sakkas–Urquhart](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/95566/1/Accepted-Version.pdf) · [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/attention-prop-traders-the-first-half-hour-of-trading-predicts-the-last-half-hour/) · [Quantitativo ES/NQ](https://www.quantitativo.com/p/intraday-momentum-for-es-and-nq) · [CXO skeptical review of the SPY variant](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/complex-intraday-time-series-momentum-strategy-applied-to-spy/)

## 2. Overnight vs intraday return split / overnight drift

**Claim(s).** (a) Virtually all of the equity premium accrues overnight; intraday (open-to-close) returns are ~zero or negative across global indices ([Knuteson, arXiv 1912.01708](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.01708), [arXiv 2010.01727](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.01727) — provocative, unexplained, and dismissed by most academics as market-structure artifact rather than manipulation, but the *pattern* is real). (b) The clean futures version: [Boyarchenko, Larsen & Whelan, "Overnight Drift," **RFS 2023** (36:3502)](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/36/9/3502/7076616) — in ES futures 1998–2019, almost 100% of the equity premium is earned **02:00–03:00 ET, at the European open**, ~3.6–3.7% annualized from that single hour. (c) Cross-sectionally, anomaly returns split sharply between overnight and intraday legs with opposite signs ([Lou, Polk & Skouras, JFE 2019, "A Tug of War"](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/TugOfWar.pdf); [Bogousslavsky, JFE 2021](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2869624) — margin/lending-fee constraints push arbitrageurs to unwind before the close).

**Mechanism / who pays.** Inventory risk transfer: dealers absorb the imbalanced closing flow at the US close (index rebalancing, MOC orders), carry inventory overnight, and are compensated when the first deep liquidity pool arrives — the European open. Sharpest fact: the effect is **asymmetric** — after US sell-offs, the 02:00–03:00 window reverses ~20% of the closing loss; after rallies the reversal is modest. The payers are US closing-liquidity demanders; the collectors are overnight inventory carriers. This is a genuine liquidity-provision premium, not folklore.

**Decay status: EXPLICITLY DECAYED — the flagship documented decay of this survey.** [NY Fed, "The Disappearing Overnight Drift," Liberty Street Economics, July 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/the-disappearing-overnight-drift/) + companion [SSRN paper](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7035838): the 02:00–03:00 window — previously >60% of ES's 5.9% annualized close-to-close return — has averaged **~zero since 2021**. The authors test three channels in an inventory-risk framework: shrinking dispersion of closing imbalances, lower return variance, and improved intermediary risk-bearing capacity. Separately, naive close-to-open ETF implementations were already killed by costs ([Alpha Architect: trading costs wipe out the overnight anomaly](https://alphaarchitect.com/trading-costs-wipe-out-the-overnight-return-anomaly/)), and buy-close-sell-open SPY was negative in 2022. **What is *not* yet established in print: whether the *conditional* (post-selloff) leg also died — the unconditional mean going to zero is consistent with the risk-compensated conditional leg surviving at lower frequency.** That open question is directly testable with our 2023–2026 1-min data and is arguably our single best research target.

**Fit for us.** The action is at 02:00–03:00 ET — immediately *before* our London window opens. Worth testing: extend the window boundary, or trade the 03:00–06:00 continuation/fade conditional on the prior US close's direction, closing-imbalance proxies, and the 02:00–03:00 realized move. Our sample is entirely post-decay, which is exactly what's needed to adjudicate.

**Sources:** [NY Fed Staff Report 917](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr917) · [Liberty Street 2021 post](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2021/05/the-overnight-drift-in-us-equity-returns) · [Liberty Street 2026 "Disappearing"](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/the-disappearing-overnight-drift/) · [Elm Wealth "Night Moves"](https://elmwealth.com/night-moves-overnight-drift/) · [Lou–Polk–Skouras](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X19300650) · [Bogousslavsky JFE 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X21000854) · [Knuteson site](https://bruceknuteson.github.io/spy-day-and-night/)

## 3. Time-of-day seasonality in index futures

**Claim.** Volume, transaction intensity, and volatility are U-shaped within each regional session (Asia/Europe/US), with spikes at regional opens/closes — the modern 24h-futures version of Wood–McInish–Ord (1985)/Admati–Pfleiderer (1988). Documented rigorously in [Andersen, Bondarenko, Kyle & Obizhaeva, "Intraday Trading Invariance in the E-mini S&P 500"](https://pages.nes.ru/aobizhaeva/ABKO-intradayinv.pdf). On *returns*: [Bondarenko & Muravyev, "Market Return Around the Clock: A Puzzle" (JFQA; SSRN 2020)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3596245) — in ES 2006–2018, a **~4-hour window bracketing the European open (starting ~11:30 p.m.–midnight ET) carries the entire average market return, Sharpe ~1.6 net of costs; the other ~20 hours average ~zero**. The pattern follows the *European* clock through DST shifts (clean causal fingerprint), and sits at the Asia-close/Europe-open handoff (mainland China closes 2:00 a.m. ET as Frankfurt/London open; HK closes 3:00 a.m. ET).

**Mechanism / who pays.** Same inventory/handoff economics as §2, plus regional information processing: when US investors are absent, European investors do the price discovery at their open. Volume/vol seasonality itself is coordination (Admati–Pfleiderer liquidity clustering) — not an edge, but a first-order *conditioning variable*: impact costs, fill quality, and optimal trade scheduling all inherit the U-shape.

**Evidence strength.** Return concentration: JFQA-published, replicated conceptually by the NY Fed work — strong. Volume/vol diurnals: unimpeachable, stable, and *not* subject to crowding decay (they're a market-structure fact, not an anomaly).

**Decay status: return leg decayed with §2 (post-2021, per NY Fed); volume/vol seasonality NOT decayed and won't be.** No published post-2021 re-estimate of Bondarenko–Muravyev specifically, but its core hour is the one the NY Fed shows flat since 2021 — treat the return claim as historical until re-verified in our sample.

**Fit for us.** Directly our window. Even if the drift is dead, the *deterministic vol/liquidity schedule* (02:00–03:00 open spike → mid-Europe lull → 08:00+ London/NY overlap ramp; thin NQ book 03:00–06:00 with higher per-contract impact) should parameterize everything we run: entry windows, stop widths, sizing. Build all session clocks in **CET, not ET**, per the DST result.

**Sources:** [ABKO invariance paper](https://pages.nes.ru/aobizhaeva/ABKO-intradayinv.pdf) · [Bondarenko–Muravyev SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3596245) · [MSU summary](https://broad.msu.edu/news/sweet-dreams-broad-research-reveals-best-times-for-overnight-market-returns/) · [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/market-return-around-the-clock/) · [NY Fed disappearing-drift](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/the-disappearing-overnight-drift/)

## 4. Opening range breakout (ORB)

**Claim.** Price exceeding the first N minutes' range predicts continuation to the close (Crabel 1990 lineage — practitioner folklore at origin).

**What published tests actually show.** (i) [Holmberg, Lönnbark & Lundström, Finance Research Letters 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612312000438) (long-sample US futures, 1983–2011): ORB profitable in the *full* sample but **not robust across subperiods** — the classic pattern of a regime artifact. A follow-up ("[Timely Opening Range Breakout on Index Futures Markets](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331076454_Assessing_the_Profitability_of_Timely_Opening_Range_Breakout_on_Index_Futures_Markets)") extends to index futures with similar fragility. (ii) [Zarattini & Aziz 2023 (SSRN 4729284 lineage)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4729284): 5-min ORB on QQQ 2016–2023, 1,484% vs 169% B&H — **with 4x leverage, single-group, not peer-reviewed**; [CXO Advisory's review](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/day-trading-with-an-opening-range-breakout-strategy/) flags assumption/cost sensitivity and single-source replication. Their stronger paper restricts ORB to "stocks in play" (top relative volume), Sharpe ~2.4 — note that's a *stock-selection* result, not an index result. (iii) **Mesfin (MNQ, 2026): ORB variants included in the falsification grid; none survive 2-pt friction.**

**Mechanism / who pays.** Weakly identified. Claimed payers: stop-driven and late reactors to overnight information; plausibly overlaps with the gamma-flow channel of §1 on trend days. The absence of a sharp counterparty story is itself a red flag relative to §§1–2.

**Decay status: never robustly established on index futures in peer review, so "decay" is the wrong frame — the honest label is UNPROVEN with published subperiod fragility (2013) and a directly on-instrument negative result (2026).** The positive recent evidence is concentrated in one practitioner group.

**Fit for us.** A "London ORB" (range from 03:00 open) is cheap to test and is the natural vehicle for the Concretum noise-area machinery transplanted to the European open. Expect the cost hurdle to bite hard: NQ's European-hours book is thin, and the falsification study says the naive version is dead. Only worth running with vol-scaled ranges, session-aware sizing, and honest friction.

**Sources:** as linked above + [Concretum papers index](https://concretumgroup.com/papers/) · [QuantConnect stocks-in-play replication](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/)

## 5. Short-horizon mean reversion

**Claim.** (a) Cross-sectional: weekly/monthly loser-minus-winner reversal ([Lehmann 1990; Jegadeesh 1990] — 1.79%/week, 2.49%/month gross in original samples). (b) Index-level daily: since ~2000 the S&P/NDX daily autocorrelation flipped negative → RSI-2/Connors-style dip-buying (practitioner canon, [QuantifiedStrategies backtests](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/rsi-2-strategy/)). (c) Futures: weekly reversal exists in futures panels ([Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/short-term-reversal-with-futures)).

**Mechanism / who pays.** Liquidity provision: impatient traders pay immediacy premia; reversal profit is the market-making fee earned in position form. Corollary: as HFT liquidity supply exploded, the fee compressed — cross-sectional STR net profitability collapsed decades ago (well documented across the anomaly-decay literature, e.g. ~50% post-publication alpha decay in [McLean–Pontiff-style evidence](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.11913)).

**Decay status: cross-sectional STR — LARGELY DECAYED net of costs (consensus). Index-level daily MR — regime, not law: it held up post-2000 including recent adaptations, but it's an equity-index regime that already flipped once (pre-2000 the index *trended* daily); assume it can flip back. Intraday OHLCV mean reversion on MNQ — explicitly falsified net of costs (Mesfin 2026, including his MGC mean-reversion configs).** The interesting recent wrinkle is [Baltussen–Da–Soebhag's End-of-Day Reversal (2024)](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/EOD.pdf): last-30-min cross-sectional reversal driven by retail attention + short-seller risk management — evidence that *new* reversal pockets appear where structural flows concentrate.

**Fit for us.** Daily-granularity MR (buy N-day NQ pullbacks, exit on strength) is testable with our data and the cost hurdle is small at daily holding periods; but it's crowded and regime-fragile. Intraday MR standalone: negative prior. Best use is as an *entry-timing overlay* on other signals (the literature note that a short-horizon MR signal, unprofitable standalone, improved net performance of an intraday trend strategy as an execution overlay).

**Sources:** [Quantpedia STR-in-stocks](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/short-term-reversal-in-stocks) · [Quantpedia STR-with-futures](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/short-term-reversal-with-futures) · [RSI-2 guide/backtests](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/rsi-2-strategy/) · [EOD Reversal SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5039009) · [Mesfin MNQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04004)

## 6. Trend following at intraday horizons

**Claim/reality.** Daily-horizon TSMOM (Moskowitz–Ooi–Pedersen 2012; CTA canon per [AQR "Demystifying Managed Futures"](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/Demystifying-Managed-Futures.pdf)) operates at 1–12 *month* lookbacks. At intraday horizons the signal-to-noise ratio collapses: per-trade expected moves shrink toward the bid-ask spread while trade count multiplies costs. There is essentially **no credible published evidence of profitable generic sub-daily trend following in index futures net of costs**; Huang et al. (2020) question even daily TSMOM's breadth. Mesfin's MNQ study is the direct falsification for the naive intraday version.

**Documented exceptions.** (i) Open→close **intraday time-series momentum** (§1) — the one intraday "trend" with top-journal support, because it's anchored to a session boundary and a hedging-flow counterparty. (ii) Post-announcement drift around scheduled macro news (see §9/§10). (iii) "Drift bursts" exist as an econometric phenomenon ([drift burst hypothesis literature](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08974)) but frequently *reverse* — they are as much a fade signal as a trend signal.

**Mechanism / who pays.** Where intraday trend works at all, the payers are the same short-gamma hedgers and slow rebalancers as §1, or anchored/stopped-out discretionary traders around news. Absent such a flow anchor, intraday trend is a costs machine.

**Decay status: nothing to decay — the broad class never worked intraday in published tests. Treat "intraday CTA" as folklore unless anchored to a session/flow event.**

**Fit for us.** Only via session-anchored variants: London-open→NY-open continuation, or post-EU-data drift. Generic MA-crossover/Donchian intraday NQ: negative prior, already falsified on-instrument.

**Sources:** [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/Demystifying-Managed-Futures.pdf) · [Quantpedia TSMOM](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/time-series-momentum-effect) · [Mesfin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04004) · [ITSM global](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S138641812100001X)

## 7. Volatility-managed / vol-targeted positioning

**Claim.** Scale exposure by inverse recent variance → higher Sharpe (Moreira & Muir, JF 2017). Works because volatility clusters strongly but expected returns don't scale with it, so deleveraging in high-vol regimes trims variance more than return. [Man/Harvey et al., "The Impact of Volatility Targeting"](https://www.man.com/insights/the-impact-of-volatility-targeting) across 60+ assets: equity Sharpe 0.40 → ~0.48–0.51, plus materially thinner left tail.

**Critique/replication.** [Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang & Yan, JFE 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X2030132X): across 103 strategies, vol-managed versions **fail out-of-sample** in direct comparisons (spanning-regression instability; not implementable in real time); Barroso & Detzel: doesn't survive costs as a standalone *alpha*. [DeMiguel et al., JF 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.13395) partially rehabilitates a conditional multifactor version.

**Mechanism / who pays.** Not a zero-sum anomaly — it's a consequence of vol predictability plus leverage constraints. Nobody has to "pay," which is exactly why it doesn't crowd away.

**Decay status: NOT decayed — but also NOT alpha. The honest reading post-Cederburg: vol management reliably improves risk shape (tails, drawdowns, Kelly-efficiency) and unreliably improves Sharpe. It is portfolio plumbing, not edge.**

**Fit for us: high, as an overlay.** Intraday realized vol is the *most* forecastable object in our dataset (clustering + deterministic diurnal from §3). Correct transfer: per-window vol targeting — size London-window trades off a forecast combining (a) recent realized vol at the same clock hour, (b) overnight range, (c) scheduled-announcement dummies. No published intraday-futures vol-managed *alpha* exists; claim only risk-shaping benefits.

**Sources:** [Cederburg et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X2030132X) · [Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/the-impact-of-volatility-targeting) · [Alpha Architect review](https://alphaarchitect.com/volatility-targeting-improves-risk-adjusted-returns/) · [Conditional vol targeting, FAJ 2020](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0015198X.2020.1790853) · [DeMiguel et al. JF 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.13395)

## 8. Order-flow imbalance predictability

**Academic evidence.** [Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov, "The Price Impact of Order Book Events" (J. Fin. Econometrics 2014)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1712822): price changes are ~linear in best-quote OFI with slope ∝ 1/depth; robust across stocks and timescales — but this is overwhelmingly **contemporaneous** explanation at ~10-second aggregation. [Cont, Cucuringu & Zhang, "Cross-Impact of OFI" (Quantitative Finance 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.13213): multi-level "integrated OFI" explains impact best; cross-asset OFI adds *forecasting* power — but predictive R² is small and **decays within minutes**. Deep-learning LOB literature (DeepLOB, Sirignano–Cont "universal price formation") shows statistically real predictability at horizons of ticks-to-tens-of-seconds, gross of costs — an HFT franchise, not a strategy for us. [Fed note on Treasury OFI (Nov 2025)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/order-flow-imbalances-and-amplification-of-price-movements-evidence-from-u-s-treasury-markets-20251103.html) confirms the amplification mechanics in futures-adjacent markets.

**What retail-grade footprint/CVD supports.** CVD/footprint content ([Bookmap](https://bookmap.com/blog/how-cumulative-volume-delta-transform-your-trading-strategy), GoCharting, etc.) is **practitioner folklore with no peer-reviewed predictive validation**; even the vendor literature concedes CVD "isn't predictive alone" and divergence signals lack a dependable edge. The academic analogue (signed *trade* imbalance) is a weaker predictor than book-based OFI. Important honest distinction: OFI *explains* returns superbly and *predicts* them barely.

**Mechanism / who pays.** Mechanical price formation: aggressive takers pay impact; queue depletion signals fragility. The 1/depth slope is the structurally useful part: **in the thin European-hours NQ book, a given imbalance moves price more** — this is a documented fact, not a hypothesis.

**Decay status: sub-second/seconds-horizon OFI alpha is professionally arbitraged (permanently crowded); the minutes-horizon residual was always small. Nothing here "decayed post-publication" — it was never harvestable at retail latency.**

**Fit for us.** With depth + CVD from June 2025+ (~14 months — enough for microstructure statistics, not for calendar anomalies): (a) execution overlay — entry timing, passive vs aggressive choice, London-hours impact model; (b) *state* variable — depth-adjusted CVD divergence as a filter/confirmation on §2/§3 session trades; (c) proxy for closing-imbalance conditioning in the overnight-reversal test. Not a standalone minutes-ahead alpha.

**Sources:** as linked · [OFI topic survey](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/order-flow-imbalance-ofi-7dff1686-44cf-4cf4-a602-b24df2b7c56e) · [Cross-impact QF 2023 full text](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14697688.2023.2236159)

## 9. Calendar/seasonal effects still standing

**Turn-of-month — STANDING, diminished.** [Xu & McConnell](https://www.chesler.us/resources/academia/turn_of_the_month_stock_returns.pdf): 1987–2005, effectively all excess equity return in the 4-day TOM window; persists in [19/20 countries](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057521903000073); confirmed in samples through [2020 (funds)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9403956/) and [UK indices 1990–2025](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/4106003.pdf?abstractid=4106003&mirid=1); [QuantSeeker 2024 review](https://www.quantseeker.com/p/turn-of-the-month-strategies-do-they): still works, more modestly. Mechanism: pension/401(k) contribution and settlement flow — a *scheduled payer* that hasn't disappeared. Best-graded surviving calendar effect.

**Pre-FOMC announcement drift — DECAYED, then possibly regime-revived: label CONTESTED.** [Lucca & Moench, JF 2015](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12196): +49 bps in the 24h pre-announcement, 1994–2011 (~80% of annual equity return). [Kurov, Wolfe & Gilbert, FRL 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1544612320315956): **essentially disappeared after 2015** (reduced uncertainty). But [recent practitioner re-tests through Dec 2024](https://www.quantseeker.com/p/trading-the-fed-the-pre-fomc-drift) find it alive conditional on high-vol environments (after-cost CAGR 8–9% on 3x ETFs, Sharpe ~0.6, in-market 5% of time). Treat as conditional/uncertainty-dependent, not a standing unconditional edge.

**FOMC-cycle even-week effect — DECAYED, mechanism defunct.** [Cieslak–Morse–Vissing-Jørgensen, JF 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12818) vs [Uppal](https://aliuppal.me/files/Ali_Uppal_CB_Cycles.pdf): fails out-of-sample, weakening from 2004 when biweekly Board meetings (the leak channel) ended; pre-2004 result outlier-driven; international version vanishes controlling for announcement effects. **Do not trade.**

**OPEX week — WEAKENING.** [Stivers & Sun, JBF 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378426613003051): large-cap OPEX-week return 0.45% vs 0.12% other weeks (1996–2008); mechanism = front-month delta-hedge unwind. [Recent backtests](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/the-option-expiration-week-effect/) ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/option-expiration-week-effect)): weak in the last ~3 years — plausibly structurally diluted by 0DTE/weeklies fragmenting expiry-linked hedge flow. Partial decay, mechanism migrating rather than vanishing.

**Fit for us.** These are *conditioning dummies*, not standalone systems, at our frequency: TOM and FOMC-day flags should enter any London-window model as regime features (the original Gao result is *stronger* on macro-news days).

## 10. European-hours specifics for US index futures (highest-value bucket)

What the evidence actually supports, ranked by credibility:

1. **The European open is the structural event of our window.** Two independent top-journal literatures (§2 RFS, §3 JFQA) converge: 02:00–03:00 ET (Frankfurt/London open, mainland-China close) is where US equity futures historically earned essentially their entire premium, with a clean DST-based causal fingerprint tying it to the *European* clock, and a mechanism (US-close inventory unwind into the first deep liquidity pool) that names the payer. **Unconditional version: decayed post-2021 (NY Fed, July 2026).** The conditional, asymmetric leg — *after US-close sell-offs, ~20% of the closing loss historically reversed in this window* — is the strongest single documented effect ever recorded in our trading hours, and its post-2021 survival is unresolved in print. This is a genuine research gap our dataset can close.
2. **Liquidity/impact structure.** The invariance literature (§3) plus OFI slope ∝ 1/depth (§8) jointly imply European-hours NQ has *higher impact per contract and per imbalance unit* — quantifiable precisely with our June 2025+ depth data. Consequence: any London-window edge must be sized to the thin book, and imbalance signals are mechanically "louder" (higher signal, but also higher cost).
3. **Scheduled European information flow lands inside our window** — German releases ~02:00 ET, flash PMIs ~03:15–04:00 ET, UK data ~02:00 ET, Eurozone aggregates ~05:00 ET ([release-time references](https://www.investing.com/economic-calendar/hcob-eurozone-composite-pmi-1491)). The macro-announcement literature (Gao's news-day amplification; Savor–Wilson-type announcement premia; Kurov's uncertainty channel) supports vol-regime and drift effects around scheduled news, but **no published paper specifically prices EU-data windows in US index futures — uncrowded, testable, non-obvious.**
4. **Retail-quantified session folklore** — "London sweep" of the Asia high/low then NY reversal, London-level breakout stats ([edgeful](https://www.edgeful.com/blog/posts/trading-sessions-explained), [NQ Stats](https://nqstats.com/), 2,700+ day samples) — is data-backed but retail-grade (no cost modeling, no multiplicity control). Status: hypothesis generator only. It is, however, structurally *consistent* with the academic session-handoff mechanism, which is more than most folklore can say.
5. **Negative prior on naive signals in this window** — Mesfin's MNQ falsification spans 2021–2025 24h data; nothing OHLCV-naive survived.

# Ranked Shortlist: Best Evidence-to-Crowding for Our Window and Data

**1. Conditional European-open inventory reversal (post-selloff drift into/through the London open).** Best pedigree of anything in our hours (RFS 2023 + NY Fed), a named payer (US closing-liquidity demanders), a named collector (overnight inventory carriers), and a *published decay* that ironically improves the evidence-to-crowding ratio: the headline unconditional effect is publicly dead since 2021, so tourists have left, while the risk-compensated conditional leg (~20% reversal of closing losses at the European open) is exactly the component an inventory model says should persist. Testable NQ expression: on days when the prior US RTH close was in the bottom quantile of returns (optionally: elevated MOC/closing-imbalance proxy, or our CVD into 16:00), enter long between 02:00–03:00 ET, exit 04:00–06:00 ET or on a vol-scaled target/stop; benchmark the same rule unconditionally to isolate the asymmetry; 2023–2026 sample is entirely post-decay, giving an honest verdict. Expected trade count is low (quantile-conditioned), so the thin-book cost hurdle is amortized over large expected moves.

**2. Session-anchored intraday time-series momentum, transplanted to the London window.** The Gao/Baltussen ITSM family is the only intraday "trend" with top-journal, multi-market, mechanism-identified support, and its US expression is anchored to the US close — nobody publishes the European-session analogue, which is the uncrowded seam. Testable expression: predictor = overnight (Asia-session) return plus first 30 minutes post-European-open (03:00–03:30 ET) return; target = remainder of the London window (03:30–06:00) and, separately, the 06:00→09:30 carry into the NY open; per Li–Sakkas–Urquhart, condition on low-liquidity/high-vol states where ITSM is strongest, and on EU-data days (Gao's news-day amplification). Sign matters: test continuation *and* reversal, since the US-close gamma mechanism doesn't obviously operate at 06:00 ET.

**3. Scheduled European macro-release windows as drift/vol events.** Flash PMIs, German/UK prints, and EZ aggregates land at fixed clock times inside our window; announcement-window effects are among the most robust facts in empirical finance at the daily level (and the pre-FOMC drift's conditional revival shows announcement premia are uncertainty-dependent, not gone), yet no published work prices these specific windows in US index futures — near-zero crowding in NQ at 04:00 ET. Testable expression: event-study NQ 1-min returns/vol in ±30 min around each recurring EU release 2023–2026; then two candidate rules — (a) post-release drift continuation for 15–60 min conditional on surprise direction (proxy surprise by the first 1-min reaction if no consensus feed), (b) pre-release vol-crush positioning to *avoid* holding other systems' positions through the print. Even a null result upgrades every other system via clean event dummies.

**4. Per-window volatility targeting + time-of-day scheduling as the sizing layer for everything above.** Not alpha (Cederburg's OOS critique is accepted), but the single most reliable published result that *transfers* to intraday: vol clusters, diurnal patterns are deterministic, and Man/Harvey-style scaling demonstrably thins left tails — which for a small shop trading a leveraged product in a thin session is survival, not decoration. Testable expression: forecast London-window realized vol from (i) same-clock-hour realized vol over trailing 20 sessions, (ii) overnight range, (iii) EU-release and FOMC-day dummies; size all window trades to constant forecast dollar-vol; validate that scaled versions of strategies 1–3 have materially better drawdown/Sharpe stability 2023–2026 than unscaled. Crowding is irrelevant since nobody pays for this — it's engineering.

**5. Depth-adjusted order-flow state as a filter (not a signal) on the June 2025+ subsample.** The academic evidence is honest: OFI explains contemporaneous moves superbly, predicts minutes-ahead barely, and the fast horizon is permanently owned by HFT — so the correct expression at our latency is conditioning, where crowding is minimal because everyone else uses this data to race, not to filter. Testable expression: build integrated multi-level OFI and depth-normalized CVD for the London window; test whether strategy-1 reversals and strategy-2 continuations have materially different hit rates conditional on (a) book-depth regime (thin vs normal for that clock hour), (b) CVD-price divergence at entry; simultaneously fit the 1/depth impact coefficient by hour to set size and passive/aggressive execution policy. Fourteen months of data is enough for microstructure conditionals (thousands of window-hours), not for standalone anomaly claims — scope it accordingly.

**Explicitly not shortlisted:** naked London ORB (on-instrument falsification, fragile published record — only worth testing as a variant inside #2's framework), generic intraday trend following (never worked in print), cross-sectional short-term reversal (decayed, and we trade one instrument), FOMC-cycle even-weeks (mechanism defunct), unconditional overnight drift (documented dead since 2021), CVD divergence as standalone signal (folklore, no peer-reviewed support).
