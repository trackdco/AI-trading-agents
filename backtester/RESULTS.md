## Where the edge actually is — Setup B + CVD divergence

Setup B (continuation, rejection off a midline in the HTF-bias direction) is the profitable
core, and **the CVD divergence layer roughly doubles its profit factor:**

| Setup-B config | trades | win% | PF | net $ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3min, **CVD divergence (mode b)**, conf 5, fixed100 | 14 | 50% | **2.80** | +2,835 |
| 3min, CVD divergence (mode b), conf 4, fixed100 | 15 | 47% | 2.73 | +2,756 |
| 3min, CVD divergence (mode b), conf 5, dynamic | 14 | 50% | 2.72 | +2,711 |
| 3min, **no CVD**, conf 4, fixed100 (same setup) | 45 | 31% | 1.38 | +3,314 |

CVD divergence cuts the trade count ~3× but lifts PF from 1.38 → ~2.7 and win rate 31% → ~48%
— direct evidence the confirmation layer works *where the setup has edge*. The result is stable
across every target mode (fixed 80/100/120, dynamic, trail80 all PF ≥ 2.3). Sample is small
(14–15 trades) — in-sample, treat as a lead, not proof.

At the *all-setups* level every CVD mode is negative because B2 dominates the trade count and
CVD can't rescue a broken setup — it only filters. CVD's value shows up once you look *within*
Setup B (above). This is the key lesson: **CVD is a confirmation layer, not a fix for a setup
with no edge.**

## What this says (and does not say)

* The engine is faithful and correct; the numbers are real.
* The mechanical translation of the *reversal* and *POC-limit* setups does **not** carry the
  edge a discretionary trader gets from them. The *continuation-with-bias* setup does, and CVD
  divergence sharpens it.
* Levers to try next are all config toggles (no BB dropped, no sub-1:1 targets): disable B2 /
  revisit its stop definition; require CVD divergence on B; tighten the confluence proximity so
  4-confluence means 4 *distinct* levels, not clustered ones (raw candidate count is ~76/day,
  far too permissive — the selectivity, not the target, is the weak link). These are decisions
  for the desk, not changes to make unilaterally.
