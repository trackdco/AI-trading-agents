Our history

In 2026, Crossroads celebrated 30 years of God’s faithfulness.

Thirty years

  1. 11 February 1996

    A group of former ANU students and their families start a new Canberra church called Crossroads, with Dave McDonald as founding pastor. It has no building and isn’t steeped in tradition. It is young, contemporary, and completely committed to university ministry. The morning meeting is at MacGregor Hall; in the evenings Crossroads meets at the Manning Clark Lecture Theatre at ANU, later demolished and rebuilt as Manning Clark Hall in the Kambri Cultural Centre, where Crossroads City still meets.

  2. June 1999

    Moore College’s Moore News reports on the young church. Read the issue in the Moore archives.

  3. 20 March 2020

    On the eve of COVID lockdowns in Canberra, Crossroads City can’t meet in Manning Clark Hall and meets at Merici College in Braddon instead. It is the last time we meet in person for almost nine months. Social distancing wasn’t really a thing yet.

  4. 2021

    Crossroads celebrates 25 years, during lockdown. Founding pastor Dave McDonald talks with Marcus Reeves, the current Senior Pastor, about the work of Crossroads and what the future holds.

  5. 2026

    Thirty years of God’s faithfulness. To mark it we hold a special week of evangelism, inviting friends, family and people from across Canberra to discover the hope found in the Lord Jesus.

Hear the story told

Senior Pastor Marcus Reeves reflects on the first 30 years and looks ahead to the next 30, as we keep working, under God, to win Canberra for Christ. And founding pastor Dave McDonald on the first 25.
