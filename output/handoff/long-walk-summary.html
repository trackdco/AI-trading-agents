Read this carefully — it's the whole game

The two biggest leaks are almost exactly equal: ~$110k from trading on days we should have sat out (a when problem), and ~$101k from picking the wrong book when we did trade (a which problem). We've spent months attacking the which (features, agent book overrides) — and it's paying, that's the +$136k captured column. But the when pool is just as large and we only just found a lever for it: the inventory stand-down gate (separate report) directly targets that $110k. The "missed" pool ($49k) is the smallest and is the cost of caution — pushing stand-down harder trades some of the $110k leak for a bigger missed pool, so the inventory gate's job is to cut the first without inflating the third.

Caveat: these pools are "recoverable if we decided perfectly," which is unreachable (that's the oracle). They're a map of where the money sits, not a promise of what we'll capture. But the relative sizes are the signal: stand-down and book choice are the two big fronts, roughly equal.

Chained's +$3,552 edge is 100% better decisions on 22 days

Identical 127 days, identical books available. The two arms chose the same action on 105 of 127 days (identical P&L on those by construction). The entire +$3,552 gap comes from the 22 days they decided differently — chained won 12 of those, lost 7, and netted +$3,552. It reads 39% vs 35%, and it also trims the stand-down leak (−$13,936 vs −$17,168: it sat out 4 more of the both-books-lose days). Memory doesn't change how often it stands down (17% flat both) — it changes which book it picks and which specific days it sits out. That's the carried playbook_notes doing real work.

One run each, not multiple seeds — the +7pp capture edge could carry some run-to-run agent variance. What makes it credible: flat-rate and health-override numbers match exactly between arms, so the only moving part really is the book/day reasoning. A repeat chained run on a second year would bank it.

4 · Where we make more money from here

  1. The $110k stand-down leak is the freshest, biggest front. The inventory gate already showed +$12.7k mechanically by attacking exactly this pool — the obvious next build is wiring it into the live v0.7 stack.
  2. The $101k book leak is the proven front — features + agent overrides, and chained memory adds to it (+$3,552 on 2026 alone). Order-flow data (CVD) would attack this pool if it survives a powered test.
  3. 2025 remains the anchor — a single −22% year holding the 4-year total at breakeven. It's a stand-down year (its losses are the "traded when both books lose" pool), which is exactly why the inventory gate matters most here.
