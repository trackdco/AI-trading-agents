`python -m scripts.audit_run_leak output/agent_runs/2026-05-31_jn1.jsonl --sess-day 2026-05-31
 --briefings <jn1-only dir>` exits 1 with two check groups. Both are explained below.

## [A] 1 finding — jn1-0531-A3 has no passing leak_check

By design. That row records tv-trigger REFUSING to adjudicate, returning
`constraints_failed: ["leak_suspected"]`. The refusal itself is the datum, so the row
carries no passing leak_check. The candidate was then re-submitted on a cleaned chart
(`jn1_2026-05-31_A3_1042_clean.png`) and adjudicated normally; that row has a passing
leak_check.

The refusal was a FALSE POSITIVE caused by the orchestrator: accumulated position drawings
from L1 (03:12→03:51) and P2 (08:26→08:51) were still on the chart at 10:42. Every
annotation predates the decision minute, so none is foreknowledge — but an agent that
cannot place those prices in time is right to refuse. Fixed for the rest of the run:
drawings are cleared before each agent-facing capture and re-laid at day end for the
review artifact.

## [C] 26 findings — "mentions N time(s) after its decision minute"

Check C flags any HH:MM string later than the decision minute. All 26 fall into four
categories, none of which is post-decision market information:

1. **`04:00` (18 of 26) — a UTC OFFSET, not a time.** The anchored-weekly `anchor` field
   serialises as `2026-05-25 18:00:00-04:00`. The regex reads the `-04:00` tail as a
   clock time. Verified on every affected briefing.
2. **Window bounds and contract constants** — `04:59`, `09:10`, `09:29`, `09:35`, `11:00`
   in `window_state.entries`, `window_clock_mechanical`, `cutoff_note_mechanical`,
   `window_note_mechanical`. These are his own rules (window ends, the 09:10 pre-market
   cutoff, the 09:35 open buffer), constant for every day of the run.
3. **Scheduled event times** — `10:00` in `macro.blackout_events[].time_et` and in macro
   prose. The runbook licenses these explicitly: scheduled release times are known in
   advance and carry no outcome.
4. **`08:04` in `P1_0803_trigger.json` `context_extras.swept.lows[0].swept_at`** — a REAL
   defect, caught in-run by the P1 trigger agent. `scripts/context_extras.py` kept the
   final partial 2m resample bucket, presenting an unclosed bar as closed. The underlying
   1m data was correctly truncated, so no future price entered; the timestamp was one
   minute optimistic. Fixed and pushed mid-run; the P1 briefing on disk retains the
   pre-fix row as the historical record. P1's verdict was a pass on independent grounds
   and its agent stated it did not rely on the row.

## The one real leak this day, and what happened to it

Logged in the run as `row: leak_incident`. The NY_AM window-open thesis briefing (09:30)
carried orchestrator prose stating "last close 30356.50" — computed from a pandas slice
whose inclusive endpoint reached bars closing at 09:32, two minutes past the decision
minute. True close at 09:30 was 30343.00. Session high/low were identical either way.

The NY_AM window was VOIDED and re-run from its start; voided rows are preserved at
`2026-05-31_jn1.VOID-nyam-v1.json`. The corrected briefing flipped the thesis from long to
short, which is why the void was necessary rather than cosmetic. No trade was affected —
the window produced zero fills before or after. Process fix: orchestrator prose states bar
windows by CLOSE time, with an explicit exclusive end at the decision minute.
