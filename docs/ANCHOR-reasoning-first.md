# THE ANCHOR — reasoning first. His re-anchor, 2026-08-14, standing for every testing run.

His words, recorded as doctrine the day after the first full agent week. This
outranks every scoreboard in the repo.

## The objective

> We are not trying to make the agent perform better. We are trying to make it
> think and trade like me. Performance is the consequence, not the target.
> Every time this project has optimised directly for results it has drifted —
> it found real statistical patterns that were not my strategy, and I ended up
> with a validated book I don't actually trade. I don't want that again.

**The acceptance test for every change:**

- A change that makes the agent's reasoning match his more closely is a WIN,
  even if results are flat or slightly worse.
- A change that improves results while moving the reasoning away from his is a
  REGRESSION. Reject it, and say so explicitly rather than quietly keeping it
  because the numbers look good.
- Anything proposed *because it scores better* must first state what it does
  to the reasoning.

## The core structural mismatch, named by him

> I form a thesis from the higher timeframe before any trigger exists. The
> trigger is how I express a view I already hold. The agent works the other
> way round — it evaluates candidates and assembles a reason afterward. That
> inversion is why its reasoning reads foreign to me even when it lands on the
> same trade.

So: **the thesis comes first and is load-bearing.** A thesis is written when
the HTF condition forms, before any trigger is evaluated, and no trade may be
taken with no thesis already on file.

## What a thesis is — four parts, every time

1. **Which mechanism.** Rebalance to a HTF MA, tailing a rejection off one,
   or a break. *"I always know which bet I'm making."*
2. **What price is expected to do, and to where.** A claim about a
   destination, not a statement that conditions are met.
3. **Both branches at the decision point, named before it resolves.** *"I
   don't predict — I identify the decision point and hold a rule for either
   outcome."*
4. **What kills the thesis.** Structural invalidation, not just where a stop
   sits. An episode stays alive until price does the specific thing that ends
   the idea.

### His worked examples (verbatim)

**Rebalance:** *"Pre-market is overextended, touching −3 VWAP deviation, and
I'm bearish — the 1h closed below its BB MA, wicked back to retest it and
closed below again, and the 15m has been trailing under it for a while. But
even bearish, it's a safe bet price at least re-touches the 15m BB MA, and
that's a safe target even if it continues down from there. So: rebalance,
targeting the 15m MA / VWAP middle confluence. If price closes below the VAL
and holds instead, the rebalance idea is off and I'm looking for continuation
down."*

**Continuation:** *"Coming into this I'm expecting the 1h to get retested, if
not at least the 15m. The open pumped aggressively but heavily rejected VWAP,
and two consecutive 15m candles have failed to break through their BB MA. So I
want shorts off that 15m behaviour plus the VWAP middle-band rejection,
targeting the VAL. If instead a 15m closes through the MA with real
efficiency, I flip — I want the retest and I'm looking long off it."*

Note what is in those and what is not: a destination, a reason rooted in the
higher timeframes, an explicit alternative branch, a condition that ends it.
**No checklist, and no confidence score dressed up as reasoning.**

## What follows

- **Hold a read with no trade.** He regularly knows exactly what is coming and
  takes nothing because no entry presented. Log the thesis with no trade
  against it — those entries matter as much as fills.
- **Passing reasons are about the ROAD, not the pattern.** His rejections of
  technically valid setups are nearly always because the path to the target is
  blocked by a higher timeframe that just rejected, with no HTF agreement.
  *"Entering anyway is hopes and dreams."*
- **No retroactive reasoning.** The thesis is recorded when it forms and never
  edited to fit what happened. Reasoning written after the outcome is
  lookahead one layer up.
- **Conviction graded the way he talks.** Some setups are very good, some are
  light, some are technically valid and he still won't touch them. Uniform
  confidence is misrepresentation.

## The comparison protocol — sort on reasoning, not on trades

> Whether the trade matched is a detail; whether the thinking matched is the
> finding.

1. **Same reasoning.** Aligned — including when the agent took a trade he
   didn't. An agent that reasons his way and catches one he wasn't there for
   is doing its job.
2. **Different reasoning on the same setup.** THE PRIORITY FAILURE — invisible
   to any results comparison. Surface precisely where the accounts diverge:
   wrong mechanism named, right level for the wrong reason, thesis assembled
   after the trigger, invalidation stated as a stop rather than as structure.
3. **No thesis where he had one.** It never formed a view at all.
4. **A thesis where he had none.** Present these; he rules whether it is a
   legitimate read he missed or a manufactured view. Never assume either way.

**Lead with category 2.** No changes are proposed until he has read the
comparison.
