# SCOPING — what a clock-corrected rev-3 actually costs

**2026-08-07. A scoping memo, not a rebuild.** Nothing derived, nothing rebuilt, sealed
span untouched. Written after `docs/AUDIT-depth-lookahead-exposure-by-family.md` and the
rev-3 depth audit established that four of the five veto-ladder inputs read the book
~60 seconds after the decision they gate.

---

## 1. Is the corrected book "rev-3 with fixed inputs" or a new candidate?

**I hold: a NEW candidate.** The case for both readings, then why.

### The case for "rev-3 with fixed inputs"

Not a weak case, and it deserves stating properly:

- **No trading rule changes.** The window is still 08:00–09:45, the gate is still
  `wall = (W OR FAR)` plus the score-0 veto, still one position at a time, still V1
  management, still flat 1 lot, still the 9.5pt floor. Every *decision rule* is
  character-for-character what the bundle declared.
- **The defect is upstream of the strategy.** A floored timestamp is a data-layer bug.
  Fixing it is the same class of act as fixing a timezone or a price scale — nobody
  would call a DST correction a new hypothesis.
- **The correction was not chosen to improve the number.** It was forced by an external
  audit and its direction is adverse (a lookahead inflates). Re-registering every
  bug-fix as a new candidate makes fixing bugs expensive, which is a bad incentive.

### The case for a new candidate — and why it wins

- **The correction changes WHICH TRADES ARE TAKEN, not just their feature values.**
  Measured, without a rebuild, on the 749-fill matrix: **107 fills the uncorrected gate
  admitted would be vetoed, and 100 it vetoed would be admitted.** The net is +7 but the
  churn is 207. A book that shares perhaps three-quarters of its trades with the declared
  one is not the declared book with better numbers — it is a different population that
  happens to be generated by the same rule text.
- **The frozen literals are not inputs, they are FITTED VALUES.** `scorer.py` says so:
  *"Values are the medians of the declared 09:30-cut book, FROZEN HERE AS ABSOLUTES."*
  They are a function of the book. Change the book and either you re-derive them — a
  fresh fit on fit data — or you keep numbers fitted to a population that no longer
  exists. There is no third option, and both branches are new specification acts.
- **The precedent is already in the prereg, stated against itself.** §5 discloses:
  *"The 9.5 floor and the four checks were originally fitted on 2025 by the pre-rebuild
  canon. The 4-check selection null assumes they were specified independently of this
  data; they were not."* The programme has already conceded that fit-derived thresholds
  compromise the selection null. Re-deriving three more on the fit span compounds a known
  problem, and doing it silently under the old candidate's name hides the compounding.
- **The veto was SELECTED, not specified.** It is *"the one loss-filter that survived all
  three bars ... out of 13 candidates tested across two scans."* A survivor of a 13-way
  search is exactly the object whose survival must be re-established when the data
  changes. It may not survive on corrected inputs, and calling the corrected version
  "rev-3" would let a failed re-selection inherit a passing candidate's status.

**The deciding argument:** the honest test of "same candidate?" is whether the old
evidence still supports the new object. Rev-3's evidence is a 130-trade fit book, a
13-candidate veto scan, a 4-way management tournament, and a robustness sweep — **all
computed on the uncorrected book.** None of it transfers. A candidate whose entire
evidence base has to be regenerated is a new candidate whatever its rule text says.

### What that does to the sealed prereg and the two signatures

`docs/LONDON-PREREGISTRATION.md` (rev 2) has two outstanding signatures:

| | status |
|---|---|
| Rev 2 draft sign-off | **PENDING (Brake)** — rev 1's signature explicitly does not carry forward |
| §1 / §3 / §4 | **Still requires ANGUS** |
| Holdout | *"may be opened only after ANGUS sign-off, once, frozen. Draft sign-off is NOT sufficient"* |

**Neither signature is compromised, because neither has been given.** That is the one
piece of luck here: the correction lands before the sealed run, not after.

**And a distinction worth being precise about: the prereg's §1 frozen configuration is
rev 2a, not rev-3.** §1 pins window **08:00–10:00**, **V8** management, `wall` gate,
score ≥ 1 — no veto, no serialization, no V1. Rev-3 is the *alternative* the bundle asks
Angus to sign **instead**. So:

- **Rev 2a is untouched by all of this** except through its own `dep_*` columns — it uses
  the same wall gate, so its W/FAR are equally contaminated, but it has **no veto ladder**
  and therefore no `dep_thick`/`dep_resist` literals to re-derive. Its exposure is
  narrower.
- **Rev-3 was never in the prereg's frozen config**, so a corrected rev-3 does not
  invalidate a signature — it changes what one of the two options on the sign-off table
  *is*. The bundle's §6 asks Angus to "sign ONE"; one of the two choices no longer exists
  as described.

## 2. Which `LON_VETO_*` literals must be re-derived

```python
LON_VETO_THICK   = 57.0
LON_VETO_RESIST  = 29.0
LON_VETO_TRIGDENS = 8.0
LON_FAR_MIN      = 4.5     # not a veto literal, but the same problem
```

| literal | source | clock-sensitive input? | must re-derive? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `LON_VETO_THICK` | median `dep_thick` of the 09:30-cut book | **YES** — `dep_thick` moves on 721 of 749 fills | **YES** |
| `LON_VETO_RESIST` | median `dep_resist` of the same | **YES** — moves on 711 of 749 | **YES** |
| `LON_VETO_TRIGDENS` | median `trigdens_30` of the same | **No** — census/bar-fed, clock-invariant | **YES, but only via the population** — the input is unchanged; the book it is a median *of* is not |
| `LON_FAR_MIN` = 4.5 | pre-rebuild canon, per prereg §5 | **YES** — the tie-break alone moves 45 wall distances and flips FAR on 9 fills | **Ruling needed** — it predates rev-3 and was never a rev-3 median, so re-deriving it would be a *new* act, not a repair |

**I cannot size how far they move, and the reason matters.** The literals are medians of
the **130-trade book**, which is downstream of the floor, the serialization and V1 — and
that artifact exists on no branch (only `london_matrix.parquet` is committed). Computing
medians on the 749-fill matrix instead gives `dep_thick` 60.0, `dep_resist` 29.0,
`trigdens_30` 5.0 — none of which match the frozen 57/29/8, confirming the matrix is the
wrong population. **The corrected medians require the rebuild.**

What that measurement *does* establish: on the 749-fill matrix the `dep_resist` median
moves under the correction (30.0 → 29.0) while `dep_thick`'s does not. At least one
depth literal is demonstrably population-sensitive to the clock. That is a lower bound.

**Separately — an inconsistency that predates the clock.** `scorer.py` says the literals
are medians of the **09:30-cut** book; the rev-3 bundle declares the window as
**08:00–09:45**. The thresholds were fitted on one cut and applied to another. This is
independent of the correction and should be ruled on regardless.

### Is re-deriving them a new search that must be declared?

**Yes, unambiguously.** Taking a median of fit data and freezing it as a gate threshold
is fitting a parameter on the fit span. It was a search the first time — the veto was one
survivor of 13 candidates across two scans — and re-running it on corrected data is a
second search over the same space. It must be declared before it runs, with the candidate
set and the survival bars stated in advance, or the "survived all three bars" claim
becomes unfalsifiable.

The cheapest honest alternative: **keep 57/29/8 frozen and apply them to the corrected
book.** Nothing is re-fitted, nothing is re-searched, and the veto is tested as declared
rather than re-tuned. It will perform worse — the thresholds no longer sit at the
population's median — and that is the correct cost of not re-fitting. I would take this
option and let the number fall where it falls.

## 3. The anchor decision

| anchor | what it costs to produce | compliant with the 2026-08-05 ruling? |
|---|---|---|
| **fill** | free — rev-3 already produces it | **NO.** A limit fill requires price to have travelled to the order, so the read contains part of the answer |
| **p1** (fill − 1 min) | free — patched into `london_depth.main()`, verified working | **NO, but closest.** It is a proxy for the decision minute, not the decision minute |
| **t_\*** (trigger close) | **an L2 schema change** — see below | **YES. The only compliant one** |

The ruling is explicit (ANGUS, 2026-08-05, quoted in trunk's `build_l3_features_london.py`):

> *"live book will look at the literal seconds, not minutes. on a limit order, of course
> the minute before entry, flow will probably be against us because it is opposing our
> direction to get filled before running the way we want it to."*
>
> *"The moment a limit trader can act on is when the order is PLACED — the trigger
> candle's close."*

**This is a specification choice with a ruling behind it, not a free parameter.** Only
`t_*` is permitted. Choosing `fill` or `p1` for convenience would be overriding a
standing ruling, which is Angus's call and nobody else's.

### Scoping the L2 schema change

Rev-3's `london_matrix.parquet` carries 59 columns — `fill`, `entry`, `direction`, but
**no trigger timestamp**. `t_*` needs one. The chain:

1. **`build_l3_features_london.py`** subprocess-drives `london_depth` (L137-142) and can
   only hand it a matrix path. It must instead pass the trigger stamp through — either by
   adding `ts` (the trigger key, already in `PASSTHRU`) to the temp matrix it writes, or
   by calling `depth_at` in-process the way trunk's version does.
2. **`london_depth.main()`** gains a `--trig-col` argument and a third anchor block,
   mirroring the `p1_` block already patched in.
3. **`london_matrix.py`** must not drop `ts` when it writes the temp matrix.

Estimated: **one file materially changed, two touched, ~40 lines.** The larger cost is
that it invalidates the `--gate` reproduction check in `build_l3_features_london.py`,
which gates the new features against the cached `london_matrix.parquet` — that cache is
uncorrected, so the gate must be re-anchored or explicitly waived with a reason.

**The Monte Carlo is the one to watch.** P(bust) is a function of the edge estimate, and
the edge estimate is inflated by a lookahead. The half-edge row (~20% P(bust)) is already
in the bundle as the pessimistic case; if the corrected edge lands near half, that row
becomes the central estimate rather than the tail. The eval-math conclusion could invert.

**Not affected:** the three engine rulings in bundle §3 (same-order twins, day-stop units,
no-realistic-target trades) are structural questions about the engine, not numbers, and
they need answering either way.

## 5. What the sealed prereg pins, and whether a corrected book satisfies it

`docs/LONDON-PREREGISTRATION.md` §1 pins, as the **frozen configuration**:

| element | frozen value |
|---|---|
| Arm | `wall` = (W OR FAR) |
| Threshold | score ≥ 1 |
| Constraint level | uncapped + $400 day stop |
| Order lifetime | session-window-end, no distance cancel |
| Risk floor | `LON_RISK_MIN` = 9.5pt, no ceiling |
| Sizing | flat 1 NQ lot |
| Window | **08:00–10:00** Europe/London, resolved per day |
| Engine | E3 limits, **V8**, `v8_be_at_open=False`, `rr_floor` 2.0, 7d lookback |

Plus a declared forward expectation of **mean R ≈ +0.48** and an explicit resolution rule:
*"a near-miss on the declared +0.48 is not decay; a sign flip is."*

**Does a corrected book still satisfy it?**

- **Rev 2a, corrected: YES on the letter, with one caveat.** No frozen element moves — the
  clock fix changes no rule. But `W` and `FAR` are computed from contaminated `dep_*`, so
  the *population* the frozen arm selects changes. The prereg pins the **rule**, not the
  book, so it survives on the letter. The +0.48 prior does not: it was measured on the
  uncorrected book and would need restating.
- **Rev-3, corrected: NO — but it never was in §1 anyway.** Rev-3 is the alternative on
  the bundle's sign-off table, not the prereg's frozen config.

**Re-signature: required, and from both.**

- **Brake** — the rev-2 draft sign-off is already PENDING and has not been given. It
  should not be given against the current numbers, since §2's "exact numbers the single
  holdout run will report" were computed on the uncorrected book.
- **Angus** — §1 is defensible unchanged (no rule moved), but §2's numbers, the +0.48
  prior, and the §4 multiplicity count all need restating. The bundle's "sign ONE
  configuration" decision cannot be taken until the rev-3 option exists in corrected form.

**The prereg's own precedent supports re-signature rather than carry-forward.** Rev 2
required a fresh signature because *"Rev 2 changed the questions being asked"* even though
*"§1's frozen configuration is unchanged — no trading rule moved."* This correction is the
same shape: no rule moves, the answers do. By the document's own standard, that needs its
own sign-off.

## 6. Recommendation

1. **Do not sign anything against the current bundle.** The rev-3 option on the sign-off
   table does not exist as described.
2. **Land the loader fixes** on the rev-3 lineage (patch prepared and verified — 9 of 11
   `dep_*` columns reproduce trunk exactly; the 2 that differ are the tie-break, now also
   fixed on trunk).
3. **Rule the anchor first**, because it determines whether the L2 schema change is in
   scope before anything is rebuilt. My reading: only `t_*` is compliant.
4. **Keep 57/29/8 frozen** and test the veto as declared on the corrected book. Re-deriving
   them is a second search over a space that was already searched 13 ways, and it must be
   declared in advance if it happens at all.
5. **Open it as a new candidate** with its own prereg and ledger line, citing rev-3 as the
   predecessor and stating plainly that its evidence base did not transfer.
