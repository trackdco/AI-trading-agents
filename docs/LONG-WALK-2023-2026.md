Angus's ask, verbatim intent: *"an overnight task… get it to test straight from the
agents, run fully from the beginning of 2023 all the way to 2026 and see how it

## The pre-registered thesis (written before the run, like everything now)

If the daily journal loop works, the walk should show: reads improving from a 2023
cold start toward 2026 maturity; capture following reads; standing rules forming,
paying, and retiring; lock-in incidents caught by fresh-eyes divergence rather than
month-end autopsy. **If reads do NOT improve with journal maturity, the
learning-loop thesis is wrong** and the design pivots to static per-regime playbooks
+ the analog table (memory-free judgment). Either outcome is worth the tokens.

## Mechanics

- **Serial within an arm** (the chain is the experiment), **arms in parallel**
  (incumbent-vs-fresh-eyes is one arm internally; prompt/sizing variants are
  separate arms, each its own sealed chain).
- ~890 sessions × (incumbent + fresh-eyes) ≈ **~1,800 agent verdicts** per arm,
  plus disagreement adjudications. Wall-clock estimate **10–15h per arm**,
  unattended, notification on completion. Checkpointed quarterly.
- **Quarterly report cards emitted as it walks** (not just at the end): reads 3-way
  + binary, capture, arm vs champion vs oracle+SD, in dollars + points +
  percentages (Angus's standing format). Journal snapshot archived every quarter so
  the evolution of its beliefs is readable afterward — which rules it wrote, when,
  what each cost or paid.
- Champion baseline for comparison is the calendar-aware rerun (post-G1 numbers),
  not the calendar-blind ledger.

## Readout Angus gets at the end

1. The year-over-year curve: reads and capture by quarter, 2023 → 2026 — the
   thesis in one chart.
2. The journal's autobiography: standing rules timeline (formed → paid/cost →
   retired), lock-in incidents and whether fresh-eyes caught them.
3. The money: arm vs champion vs oracle+SD by year, full history, one table.
4. Verdict against the pre-registered thesis, stated plainly.
