Correction, before anything was acted on

The first version of this document concluded “the short-timeframe trigger family is closed” from the raw result. That conclusion was wrong and is withdrawn. The objection that produced the correction: the raw book is unselected substrate — a population full of trades nobody would take — so raw-versus-raw cannot settle which trigger timeframe supports the better traded book.

The objection is right. The test is below. What survives unchanged: every gate, every raw number, the refutation of predictions 3 and 5, the stop-rule defect, and the verdict on the momentum condition. What is withdrawn: the closure of the family, and the cancellation of the cross-timeframe union.

Answer two — at matched selectivity

Hold trades per day constant and compare. The same three filters at every timeframe — a 2-point risk floor, room-to-run of 3R or open space, then top flow concordance until the rate hits target. Selectivity is swept, not tuned. Only the timeframe changes.

The raw ordering reverses. At every rung from one to four trades a day, 3m and 5m beat the control by +0.12 to +0.20R. At the 2-a-day rung the intervals clear zero in both eras at 2m, 3m, 5m and 15m — and not at 1m. Split-half on the frozen day-split: all five positive on both halves, 5m the steadiest at +0.435 / +0.418.

The advantage lives exactly where you operate. As selectivity loosens the books converge and the 15-minute book improves — +0.256 → +0.328 — while the short-timeframe books decay. The raw comparison was measuring a twenty-trades-a-day regime that nobody trades.

Why — and this is the useful part

The reversal is carried entirely by one filter. Remove room-to-run and the raw ordering returns intact:

room + concordance no risk floor+0.256+0.421+0.418+0.304
risk floor + concordance no room+0.050+0.168+0.165+0.234
concordance only+0.043+0.169+0.165+0.234

The mechanism is arithmetic, and it explains the refutation of prediction 3 instead of contradicting it. At 15 minutes the median next level sits only 0.40R away — the stop is so wide that everything is close by in R terms, so a room filter has almost nothing to select. At 3 and 5 minutes the same distances are 0.83R and 0.67R with a far longer right tail, and the filter has real range to work in.

The tighter stop’s payoff is not a bigger move. It is that room-to-run becomes a measurable, selectable quantity. Prediction 3 said the excursion table would shift up. It did not, and it still has not. What shifts is the selectability of the population.

What this is not

Fit-side and post-hoc. The filter stack was assembled after seeing the raw result. Room-to-run was declared a reported column and explicitly not a filter this pass; concordance failed its own gate bar. Two of the three filters are being used in ways their own declarations forbade for this pass. The split-half passes, but it was run on an object chosen by looking — a stability check, not a confirmation.

Nothing here ships. What it does is reopen a family this document had wrongly closed, and hand the next declaration a specific, mechanism-backed hypothesis instead of a vague one.

Prediction 3 is the one that matters. The premise was: same excursion, smaller denominator, therefore a bigger R multiple. Why the raw book loses

Two mechanisms, both measured, and only one of them is about cost.

Only the 15-minute book survives an adverse-fill assumption. But cost explains roughly a third of the gap, not the whole of it.

2 — The stop rule itself degenerates

“Stop at the trigger candle’s extreme, plus a tick” is a sound risk unit when the candle is 30 points tall. At 1 minute the candle’s own range is sometimes a single point — and then risk stops being a risk unit. The stop sits inside the noise, and cost as a fraction of R explodes.

This is a defect in the stop rule at short timeframes, not a fact about short timeframes — and it is separable, which matters for what comes next.

Contamination this exposes

For a long, the stop is the candle’s low, so risk ≈ closeloc × range. A candle that closed near its own stop has a near-zero denominator. closeloc is the strongest single feature in the whole flow table — Spearman +0.35 at every timeframe — and that correlation is partly mechanical. Any future use of it, including the bar-only proxy, has to be re-measured on a risk-floored population.

The momentum condition adds nothing

Condition B — the same candle closing through its own timeframe’s middle band — priced by the declared marginal-lift arithmetic, not by whether each population clears its own bar.

Bar: lift ≥ +0.05R with the bootstrap interval on the lift clear of zero in both eras. Result: 0 of 30 cells pass. The verdict recorded is the declared one — B adds nothing, the trigger stays A-only.

That stands. But the two arms failed differently and the difference is on the record:

  • Reject arm — B is a savage filter, removing 93–98% of 1-minute triggers. Point estimates are positive in 14 of 15 cells, median about +0.18R, and hit rate and expectancy agree — this is not the wall-quality inversion. It fails on power, not sign: 30–170 kept rows per cell.
  • Break arm — estimates straddle zero, 7 of 15 negative. Here B genuinely adds nothing.

The sign consistency is not a clean 15-cell sign test: the cells share populations across timeframes, so the effective independent count is far smaller. It earns a fresh declaration on the 15-minute reject book, where the kept set would be roughly seven times larger. Nothing more.

Wiring check
At 15 minutes the locus is that timeframe’s own middle band, so B must be trivially true for every break and trivially false for every reject. Observed: 100.0% and 0.0%. Both identities hold exactly, and at 1 minute the same locus gives 21.9% / 5.7% — so B is a genuinely independent condition down there, which is what made the test worth running. A side effect worth stating plainly: the incumbent 15-minute reject book is precisely the population B throws away.

The bar-window features get weaker at one minute, not stronger. The pre-bar features are flat across every timeframe — exactly as they must be, since they never used the bar window — and that is the internal control confirming the comparison is fair.

The one feature that appears to strengthen is the same number arriving twice: at a 1-minute trigger, thru_delta_conf and flowconf are 100.0% identical. Its rise is arithmetic, not evidence.

This closes a question that has been open since the beginning. The flow family is genuinely weak, not mis-measured. Aligning delta to the exact event minute does not rescue it. That is a result, and it is recorded as one.

One thing did move, honestly: the concordance count ranks the short-timeframe population better than it ranked the 15-minute one (+0.19 to +0.24 on the reject arm, against +0.129 before). It still has no gate value — 96 cut cells searched, 0 clearing the bar in both eras.

Room-to-run: how far the next level sits ahead of entry, measured in R. Declared as a reported column and explicitly not a filter this pass.

With a 2-point risk floor, as a gate with the paired interval on the lift, it clears both eras at 2m, 3m and 5m (+0.185 / +0.212 / +0.214). And the obvious confound runs against it: small stops inflate the ratio and predict bad outcomes, so the room-rich group is loaded with the worst population and beats the book anyway.

This is not a result. It was found by looking, after the fact, across a table that also holds 168 other cells. It is the candidate that has earned the next blind declaration — and it is the room-to-run law restated in the data: select on how far it can go, not on whether it arrives.

What this pass costs, and what it buys

Closed

The 1-minute trigger. It loses raw, it is worst at every matched-selectivity rung, and it is the only timeframe that fails the both-era check under selection. And the momentum condition — the stated grammar’s own confirmation requirement — adds nothing at any timeframe.

Buys

  • The flow question is closed: weak, not mis-measured. That has been open since the beginning.
  • Everything the last six months of base rates was measured on is re-validated as the right population. The invalidation list in the declaration is withdrawn.
  • A stop-rule defect is now visible that was invisible at 15 minutes — and it contaminates closeloc at every timeframe.
  • A mechanism, not just a ranking: room-to-run is worth roughly three times more at 3–5 minutes than at 15, and the reason is measurable.

Reopened

  • The 3-and-5-minute trigger family. Wrongly closed in the first draft of this document.
  • Cross-timeframe union. The cancellation was conditional on no short-timeframe book being competitive. Two are. It still needs a declared de-duplication rule before it can be built.
  • Depth at the short-timeframe decision bar — the staleness argument now has a book to select inside.

Still sealed

Holdout look #1 stays halted, but for a different reason than before. Not “the trigger set is closed” — the trigger set is reopened at 3 and 5 minutes. Spending the look now would burn it on a question the next declaration is about to reframe.

The next declaration, in one line

Blind, before any of it is measured: at 3 and 5 minutes, does a room-to-run gate clear +0.05R of marginal lift with both-era intervals, per session, at matched trades per day against the 15-minute control? Everything in “answer two” is the hypothesis, not the answer.
