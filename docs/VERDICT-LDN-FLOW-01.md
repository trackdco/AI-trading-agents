## VERDICT: no measure passes. **Minute-aggregate order flow at entry has no power to separate winners from losers in London fade events.**

| measure | 2025 ρ | 2026 ρ | AUC (25/26) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CONFIRM | −0.006 | −0.121 | 0.509 / 0.467 | INCONCLUSIVE ON POWER |
| ABSORB | +0.085 | −0.028 | 0.550 / 0.462 | INCONCLUSIVE ON POWER |
| EFFORT | +0.010 | +0.062 | 0.503 / 0.557 | **FAIL** (fragility) |
| TRAPPED | −0.023 | +0.068 | 0.467 / 0.549 | **FAIL** (fragility) |

All four were declared **positive** in advance. **Every 95% CI contains zero.** No measure
survives Holm–Bonferroni. Two fail the fragility ladder outright — their sign flips as the
lookback moves between 3, 5 and 10 minutes, which is what noise does.

**The AUC column is the number that answers the question.** AUC is the probability the
measure reads higher on a winner than on a loser. **0.5 is a coin flip.** The observed
range is 0.462–0.557. At entry, these flow measures cannot tell a winner from a loser.

## 1. Was this powerful enough to mean anything?

Yes, for effects of the size that would matter.

| era | n | minimum detectable ρ @80% |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 (Jun–Dec) | 141 | **0.209** |
| 2026 (Jan–Jul) | 106 | **0.240** |

We could have detected a rank correlation of ~0.21 and measured ~0.00. This is a null on
evidence, not on blindness. *(The prereg estimated ≈0.27 pre-run; the exact figures are
above.)*

It is **not** powered to exclude a genuinely small effect — ρ = 0.15 would have been
missed. But an effect that small cannot "remove unnecessary losers"; it would move the win
rate by roughly a point.

## 2. The finding that matters most — why two eras exist

This is the result to put in front of Angus.

**TRAPPED, 2026 only**, median split:

| half | n | mean | win rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| high | 53 | **+8.19 pts** | **56.6%** |
| low | 53 | **−12.03 pts** | 47.2% |

That is a 20-point spread and a 9-point win-rate gap — *exactly* the shape of "flow
confirmation removes unnecessary losers." On one era it looks like the answer.

**The same split in 2025 is reversed:**

| half | n | mean | win rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| high | 70 | **−2.46 pts** | 40.0% |
| low | 71 | **+4.14 pts** | 50.7% |

The filter that "works" in 2026 loses money in 2025, and the one that loses in 2026 works
in 2025. **A single-era study of this measure would have produced a confident, wrong,
tradeable-looking conclusion.** The two-era rule caught it for the cost of nothing.

Everything about this is what a false positive looks like from the inside. It is not
obviously noise on its face — it has a plausible mechanism (trapped aggressors exiting), a
believable magnitude, and a clean monotone split.

## 3. CONFIRM is weakly wrong-signed in both eras

CONFIRM — aggressive delta at the entry minute pointing the way you are about to trade,
the most literal reading of "confirmation at entry" — comes back **negative in both eras**
(−0.006, −0.121). Not significant (2026 two-sided p ≈ 0.22), so this is a direction, not a
result.

It is mechanically coherent, which is why it is worth recording rather than discarding:
on a *fade*, flow already running your way at the entry minute means the reversal has
started without you. You are joining late, at a worse price, with less of the move left.
"Wait for confirmation" and "get a good fill" are in direct tension on a mean-reversion
entry. If anything in this table is real, it is this — and it points the opposite way to
the request.

**This is a hypothesis generated by the data, not a tested result.** It would need its own
prereg to count for anything.

the means, while the rank ordering disagrees, is a handful of large events, not a filter.
It is reported because the prereg required all four reported; it is **not** a finding.

## 5. Clean-run confirmation

- **Causality (prereg §5):** every flow minute read lies in `[t−4, t]`. The outcome is
  measured over `(t, window close]`. The intervals never overlap, and the test asserts
  `win.index.max() <= t` on every event. This is the defect that killed LDN-SWP-01 and it
  was checked for explicitly.
- **Event sets unchanged:** `ldn_flow01_events.py` asserts the rebuild reproduces the
  signed-off verdict counts and means (161/−2.30, 89/−2.64, 77/−3.81, 38/−12.15) before
  anything runs. No candidate was re-specified to suit the flow test.
- **Delta sign verified, not assumed:** `delta = b − a`, correlating **+0.64** with
  same-minute (close − open) over 369,045 minutes. Positive delta = aggressive buying. A
  backwards convention would have inverted every conclusion in this document.
- **Coverage:** 248 of 365 events fall inside the flow span; **247 measured**. The single
  drop is 2026-01-02, whose entry minute is absent from `fp_minutes`. No event was
  excluded for any property of its outcome.

## 6. What this does NOT establish — read before concluding "order flow doesn't work"

This result is narrower than it looks, and overclaiming it would be its own error.

- **Minute-aggregated footprint only.** `fp_minutes` gives one bid/ask volume pair per
  minute. Real absorption reads *at a price level within* the minute — 400 contracts
  hitting one price that does not move. That signature is invisible at this resolution and
  **is not tested here**.
- **Resting liquidity is untouched.** We hold **295 days of MBP-10 depth** in
  `data/reference/depth_london/`. Book imbalance, pulled bids, iceberg refill and
  size-at-level are a **genuinely different information family** — aggressive flow versus
  passive intent. Nothing in this verdict speaks to them.
- **The substrate is two null fade candidates.** Flow was asked to find structure inside
  event sets that have no directional edge. It found none. Flow applied to an event set
  that *does* have an edge is a different question.
- **L0 structure only.** No stops, targets or costs. Flow is tested here as an information
  filter on endpoint drift, not as a trade-management input — and stop placement is where
  flow is most often claimed to earn its keep.
- Fades only. Nothing here transfers to continuation or breakout entries.

## 7. Trial accounting

**8 trials** into LDN-FLOW-01 (4 measures × 2 era directions). London programme running
total: **24** (16 prior + 8). These count in the DSR denominator per §2.4. The median-split
and per-candidate tables add no trials — they re-describe the same eight statistics.

## 8. Recommendation to Angus

**Do not wire minute-aggregate delta into London entries.** On this evidence it would add
latency, complexity and a data dependency in exchange for an AUC of 0.5.

**Do not tombstone "order flow" on this.** The tested family is minute-aggregate
aggressive volume. The two families most often meant by "order flow confirmation" —
price-level footprint within the minute, and resting-book depth — are untested and the
depth data is already on disk.

If the desk wants the next step, the honest one is **book depth, pre-registered from
scratch, on an event set chosen before the test rather than inherited from two failures**.
That is a new family and a new prereg; it is not an extension of this one. I have not
started it, because choosing the next flow family *after* seeing which one failed is how a
search launders itself into a discovery.

One process note worth carrying: the threshold-free design is what made this cheap. Testing
four measures by rank correlation cost 8 trials. The same four measures with five
thresholds each would have cost 40, and would have found a "winner" — TRAPPED in 2026 was
sitting right there.
