Monte Carlo — live paths

Every run resamples real trading days from the COMBINED shipped canons — NY (pre + golden) AND London — 225 sessions, 400 sized trades, Jun 25 – Jul 26, sized by conviction-based dollar risk: each trade risks a fixed $ per conviction tier (1.0 = $200, 1.5 = $300, 2.25 = $400 at the floor), and contracts = risk$ / (stop × $2), so a tight stop buys more micros and a fat stop fewer — every trade of a tier risks the same dollars. Full cycle = the Lucid journey: pass the $3k eval, then a fresh funded account to the $4k max-payout target. EOD drawdown (not trailing): the floor updates at day-end and locks at start balance once the lock is banked. The base $ scales up with available drawdown (+$75 per $1k past $3k), so size grows as the buffer builds and steps back down when it thins. Real Lucid economics — repeated $2k payout cycles and withdrawals — live in scripts/mc_payout_cycles.py.

Reading it

The danger zone is the left edge: before the floor locks, a $2k EOD leash sits under the book. Two things keep busting rare. First, dollar-risk sizing normalizes exposure — the same "1.0 average setup" that risked $98–$724 under a flat micro count now always risks $200, so a fat-stop day can't quietly over-bet you. Second, the base $ scales off available drawdown — every $1k of headroom adds to the base, and the same rule steps risk back down as the buffer thins, so approaching the floor mechanically shrinks the bets that could reach it. Together they cut the naked funded-year bust to ~1.5% (vs ~4.9% under static micros) before the safety spine's halts even act. Paths resample our own backtest days: sequencing risk, not model risk.
