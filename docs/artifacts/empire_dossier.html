Armed Empire Dossier

This page previously presented a seven-year validation record for a mechanical NQ program. That record was produced by a look-ahead bug and is withdrawn in full. The page is kept, corrected, because the failure is more useful than the claim was. Repo trackdco/ai-trading-agents, doc §40.

The strategy has no edge. It is not fundable and not tradeable.

The backtest scanned for stop and target exits starting on the fill bar itself. A limit order fills at an unknown moment inside a one-minute bar, but that bar's high and low also contain movement from before the fill — so every target that happened to sit within the fill bar's range was booked as a win, including runs that were already over by the time the order filled.

Caught by Brake, running the same spec against TradingView seconds data where the true intrabar path is visible: 571 trades across six days, engine +176R versus seconds +18R. Reproduced independently in our own engines by scanning exits strictly after the fill bar.

The result does not decay toward zero — it inverts, in a near-perfect mirror. The entire edge was the leak. The 65–70% win rate quoted throughout the original was the bug's most visible fingerprint; honest fills give 43%.

Why eleven gates, PBO 0.000 and a seven-year walk-forward all missed it

This is the part worth keeping. Every validation on the original page — PBO across 12,870 CSCV splits, deflated Sharpe, walk-forward efficiency 1.01, the preregistered 2020–22 holdout, and an external 11-gate audit — consumed the same trade dumps. Each of those tests asks whether a P&L series is overfit, cherry-picked, or non-persistent. None of them asks whether the P&L was computed correctly.

  • A look-ahead leak passes overfitting tests by design. The leaked series is genuinely stable and genuinely persistent across eras and folds — it simply is not obtainable. PBO 0.000 and WFE 1.01 measured a bug with great precision.
  • The independent rebuild was not independent. It was written from the same prose spec, which never stated the fill-bar convention, so it inherited the same optimistic assumption and matched trade-for-trade. Its 10-of-11 is void for the same reason.
  • Neither was the human check. Marking trades on a chart makes the identical assumption: you see the level tagged, then a run to target, and infer the fill came first. Two copies of one error agreeing is not validation.
  • The out-of-era holdout "passing" proved only determinism — a bug reproduces in every era, which is exactly what it did.
  • The most attractive result was the leak at its most concentrated. The arming rule required price to reach the target price before the entry could fill, which selected precisely the setups whose fill bar already contained the target. That is why it read as +30% expectancy for free.

What was tested after the fix

A market order at the signal candle's close has no intrabar fill ambiguity — the entry price is fixed at a bar boundary — so it is structurally leak-free and is the honest form of the question. Tested on prior-day VAH/VAL across 1, 3 and 5-minute signal candles, targets 1.0R to 3.0R, four close-through depths — 60 configurations: every one net negative, none positive in both halves of the sample. The best sit near −0.01R per trade, which is approximately zero before costs and negative after — matching the seconds-data finding independently.

Note the earlier kill of "market-style entry" was a comparison against the leaked limit baseline, so it was void too. This was its first fair test, and it is also negative.

What survives

The data pipeline, the volume-profile level computation, the news archive, the position-rail and dedupe machinery, and the practice of writing verdict rules before reading results. The engines survive with the fix committed (--exit-next-bar) and the leaked path retained only to reproduce the retracted dumps. Nothing about the edge survives.

The standing rule that comes out of this: no strategy gets a "real" verdict until its fill and exit conventions have been validated against sub-minute data. Overfitting tests are not correctness tests, and no number of them substitutes for one.
