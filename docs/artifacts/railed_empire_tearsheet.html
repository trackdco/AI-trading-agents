§19 · §34

The grammar

Every trade in every book is the same five moves. Nothing in the four years below comes from a different rule — only from a different list of levels.

SIGNAL
Close through
A 1-minute candle closes ≥3pt beyond a reference level.
ENTRY
Limit at the retest
Rest an order back at the level. No chasing — market entry is a tested loser.
RISK
Structural stop
One tick past the close-through candle. 5pt floor, 30pt cap — wider signals are never placed.
EXIT
Full out at 1R
No partials, no runners. Both were built and both lost.
FLIP
Stop and reverse
An opposing close through the same level turns the position around.

Gates: no entries 08:00–09:30 on high-impact news days · pendings die at 16:00 · flat by session end · every trade sized by its own R.

§34

The 30pt stop cap

A structural stop sits one tick past the signal candle, so a giant 1-minute candle makes a giant stop — the tail is event days (the April 2025 tariff crash ran stops of 179–270pt). Stops run 8.5pt median, 27pt at p95, 269.8pt at the extreme; 3.9% of signals asked for more than 30pt.

Those wide-stop trades are also the weakest part of the book. Under flat contract sizing that is the worst possible allocation: the most dollars on the lowest-expectancy trades.

The mistake worth keeping

The audit said the trades G3b would block earn nothing, so blocking them looked free. Run in-engine, G3b cost −414R flat. The blocked bucket was actually earning +0.258R at 72% win rate, because a rail pass is chronological: refusing one trade changes which trades are open — and therefore available — for the rest of the day.

The rule: an audit bucket is not a rule effect, whenever the rule changes what else can be open. Measure the rule, never the bucket.

in-engine · 954 days

Arming: the layer that won

The audit above says a retest only pays when price has already run a full stop's distance past the level. Arming is that finding turned into a rule — and it is the single biggest improvement anything has made to this strategy.

The rule: the limit order stays dark until price runs 1R past the level. Only then does it go live. Same levels, same stop, same target, same rails. The only change is that the order does not exist until the move has proved itself.

Frozen spec versus armed, both on the full 954-day tape
TradesPer dayPer tradeNet RR / dayMax DDSharpeGreen days

Fewer trades, more money, smaller hole. Arming throws away 19% of the trades — the shallow pokes — and still earns more R in total, because the ones it keeps earn +30% each. The drawdown drops from −18.1R to −14.0R, which is the number that actually decides how big you can trade.

Why it matters more than the R

  • It doubles carryable size. On a $2,000 trailing-drawdown funded account, the flat book carries 8 micros at ≥80% pass odds; the armed book carries 16. Same account, same rules, twice the position.
  • It is free to build. Displacement is known before the order goes live — the executor simply holds the order back. No mid-flight size change, unlike conviction sizing.
  • The ridge is monotone. Arm at 0R, 1R, 1.5R: +0.0%, +36.1%, +40.2% on the full empire. Not a lucky cell.

What it costs, honestly

  • Every year, all four. Armed beats flat in 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026 — +2,280 / +2,918 / +3,108 / +2,557.
  • 45 of 45 months still positive, worst month +20.1R.
  • Direction confirmed in three eras, magnitude in one. Found on 2023–26; replicated on 2020–22 (+29% / +34% drawdown-matched, on seen data); and on the sealed 2017–19 holdout it again raised per-trade EV (+0.070 → +0.103) and cut drawdown (−84.8 → −55.4R) — but on a tape where the base grammar itself is marginal, so the percentage there means little.
  • It does not stack with conviction sizing. The armed book is displaced by construction, so 2:1 displacement sizing on top of it is a literal no-op: +0.0%.

in-engine · 921-day tape

Conviction sizing: the other survivor

Every conditional layer before these two was tried as a filter — trade or don't trade — and every one died. This one is not a filter. It takes the same trades and moves size from the low-conviction ones to the high-conviction ones.

The rule: full size when price ran at least 1R past the level before the retest, half size when it did not. One feature, two sizes, about 70% of trades take the bigger size. Note that it reads the same feature as arming — so it is an alternative to arming, not an addition to it. Pick one.

How big, honestly

  • Block bootstrap: 97.4% of draws clear the +5% bar.

Why it's believable

  • The engine patch changes nothing. Re-run and diffed against the pre-patch dump: 22,187 trades, 0 differing fields. The flag adds labels; the frozen spec is untouched.
  • The rail pass reproduces the receipts exactly — 71,961 trades, 921 days, +9,896R, −18.1R drawdown.
  • Not free plumbing: displacement is known at fill time, so the executor must be able to raise a resting order's size mid-flight.

The guard rails

Three books firing at once will overlap. Each rail below is mechanical, and most were measured before they were written — the caps have never once bound in four years, which is the point of insurance.

G1
One per book. Max one position plus one working limit; a newer own-book signal replaces the unfilled pending.
G2
Merge crowded levels. At 18:00, levels within one stop floor collapse to the stronger family. Binds 1.3 levels a day.
G3
First in wins. No book opens a same-direction position within one stop floor of any open position. Removed 1,923 duplicate entries — which were net losers. Widening it past one stop floor was tested and lost badly.
G4

G8

§2 · §6 · §32

The honesty stack, and what to attack

Haircuts already taken

  • Fills must earn it. A limit only fills if price trades a full tick through the level. Touch fills would flatter the raw edge by roughly 40%.
  • Costs in, per contract. 0.5pt per round trip, subtracted in R against each trade's own stop.
  • Ambiguity counts as a loss. When a bar could have hit stop and target, it is scored a loss. Fill-bar stop-outs are taken.
  • No lookahead. Prior-day levels, closed candles, forward-only fills and exits.
  • Levels are computed, not read off a chart — and re-running with the measured level error injected keeps 86–89% of the edge.

Known idealisations

  • Queue position is assumed, not modelled. Stress-tested below — this is still the biggest gap.
  • The cross-book rail pass is post-hoc chronological. An exact joint simulation is executor-stage work.
  • No cost shocks, no missed sessions — which is part of why a simulated Sharpe should not be read as a live forecast.
  • ~80 trades a day is automation only. None of this is hand-tradeable, and it was never designed to be.
  • Arming's magnitude is still 2023–26 only. Its direction has held in three eras; its size (+30% per trade, −23% drawdown) has been measured cleanly on one.
  • Gold sits outside this stream — its own constants, its own volatility dial, certified separately.

The edge dies between 2 and 4 ticks. Doubling the fill requirement costs 25% of the R and 61% more drawdown — the tail rots faster than the mean, because the fills you still get are the ones where price kept running against you. What supports the 1-tick assumption: April 2026 depth data over 173,980 level observations shows median resting size of just 3 contracts, and the strategy's own levels are only ~10% thicker than the rest of the book. The feared "everyone stacks orders at the obvious level" does not appear in the data — at retail size.

One bar is 60 seconds — roughly a thousand times real network latency — so this is not about the connection. It is about the shape of the executor's loop. Requirement: the arming check must fire and the order must reach the exchange within the same minute the arming bar closes. An executor that polls once a bar and acts on the next one gives away a third of the edge.

The one thing to instrument first

Log the fill penetration of every resting limit in paper trading — how far did price actually trade through the level before the fill came back? Compare that distribution against the 1-tick assumption. It is a small amount of executor code, and it converts the largest remaining unknown in the whole program from a guess into a measurement.
