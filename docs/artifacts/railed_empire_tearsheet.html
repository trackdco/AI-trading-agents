§19 · §34

The grammar

Every trade in every book is the same five moves. Nothing in the four years below comes from a different rule — only from a different list of levels.

SIGNAL
Close through
A 1-minute candle closes ≥3pt beyond a reference level.
ENTRY
Limit at the retest
Rest an order back at the level. No chasing — market entry is a tested loser.
RISK
Structural stop
One tick past the close-through candle. 5pt floor, 30pt cap — wider signals are never placed.
EXIT
Full out at 1R
No partials, no runners. Both were built and both lost.
FLIP
Stop and reverse
An opposing close through the same level turns the position around.

Gates: no entries 08:00–09:30 on high-impact news days · pendings die at 16:00 · flat by session end · every trade sized by its own R.

§34

The 30pt stop cap

A structural stop sits one tick past the signal candle, so a giant 1-minute candle makes a giant stop — the tail is event days (the April 2025 tariff crash ran stops of 179–270pt). Stops run 8.5pt median, 27pt at p95, 269.8pt at the extreme; 3.9% of signals asked for more than 30pt.

Those wide-stop trades are also the weakest part of the book. Under flat contract sizing that is the worst possible allocation: the most dollars on the lowest-expectancy trades.

It is not free money — the skipped trades did make +198R over four years — it is a fix for an allocation absurdity. Tighter caps cost real edge: 15pt keeps only 84% of the R.

in-engine · 2026-09-03

Conviction sizing: the first survivor

Every conditional layer before this was tried as a filter — trade or don't trade — and every one died. This one is not a filter. It takes the same trades and moves size from the low-conviction ones to the high-conviction ones.

The rule: full size when price ran at least 1R past the level before the retest, half size when it did not. One feature, two sizes, about 70% of trades take the bigger size.

+1,474R over four years for the same average risk — and the worst day gets smaller, not bigger. Still 45 of 45 months positive; the worst month improves from +8.1R to +10.5R. Pre-registered bar was +5% R/day in both halves at matched drawdown; it delivered +16.3% in-sample and +17.8% out-of-sample, and +16 to +18% in every one of the four years.

How big, honestly

  • Normalise by daily volatility — the conservative floor — and it is +3.4% (Sharpe 1.158 → 1.197).
  • Normalise by drawdown and it is +17%. Tiered sizing shrinks the bad tail faster than it shrinks the mean, because it downsizes exactly the trades that cluster into bad days.
  • A funded account's binding constraint is a trailing drawdown, not a variance — so the larger number is the one that pays the bills, and the smaller one is the floor.
  • Block bootstrap: 97.4% of draws clear the +5% bar.

Why it's believable

  • The negative control failed. Sizing on session progress alone is worse than flat (−9.4%). It isn't "any tiering helps" — it's displacement specifically.
  • The engine patch changes nothing. Re-run and diffed against the pre-patch dump: 22,187 trades, 0 differing fields. The flag adds labels; the frozen spec is untouched.
  • The rail pass reproduces the receipts exactly — 71,961 trades, 921 days, +9,896R, −18.1R drawdown.
  • Not free plumbing: displacement is known at fill time, so the executor must be able to raise a resting order's size mid-flight.

Full tables, the bootstrap, and the caveats — including that these features were selected from this same four years — are in docs/FINDINGS-conviction-sizing.md.

§14–§16 · §20–§21 · §28–§29

What was tried and died

Every addition was pre-registered — the pass/fail rule written before the result was read — and the failures are logged as loudly as the survivors. Not one conditional layer survived.

killed
Trade only with the trend. A latched daily regime flag skipping counter-trend crossings. Halved the total and deepened the drawdown, in both halves of the sample — fading a stretched tape at a level is a large share of the whole edge.
killed
Partial at 1R plus a runner. Entries sit at the most-retested price on the chart, so a breakeven runner dies on 4,203 of 4,318 spawns. Every variant lost to the plain full exit.
killed
A daily loss cutoff. Made the max drawdown worse at −20, −30 and −40pt alike. Red mornings heal by the close; a cutoff prints the low.
killed
Market entry on the close-through. Negative at every timeframe and every target. Chasing pays the displacement — the resting limit is the filter.
killed
Cutting the "wrong-way" band trades. All ten band × direction cells came back positive in both halves; the feared trade — shorting a −2σ break — is the single best cell in the book, because the retest turns a chase into a pullback entry.
killed
~20 pre-entry features over three rounds. Slope alignment, band width, day of week, hour, prior result, stop width and more. Across fifty-plus buckets, nothing was negative in both halves — so nothing qualified for a cut.
kept
More level families, and the rails. The things that worked were additions, not subtractions: new books to price, and mechanical rules to stop the books doubling up on each other.

The one honest lever the feature hunt did find is allocation, not exclusion — VWAP trades earn two to four times more per trade in New York than overnight. Recorded as an option, not a rule.

§31

The guard rails

Three books firing at once will overlap. Each rail below is mechanical, and most were measured before they were written — the caps have never once bound in four years, which is the point of insurance.

G1
One per book. Max one position plus one working limit; a newer own-book signal replaces the unfilled pending.
G2
Merge crowded levels. At 18:00, levels within one stop floor collapse to the stronger family. Binds 1.3 levels a day.
G3
First in wins. No book opens a same-direction position within one stop floor of any open position. Removed 2,009 duplicate entries — which were net losers.
G4
Pending hygiene. A resting limit is pulled the moment a same-direction position opens near its price, so duplicates die before they fill.
G5
Four positions, empire-wide. The 99.8th percentile of what was actually measured; binds about 2 days in 921.
G6
Three in one direction. Binds on 0.3% of days.
G7
Open risk ≤ 4R in total, for when per-stream sizing varies.
G8
News gate. No entries 08:00–09:30 on high-impact days; pendings die at 16:00; flat by session end.
G9
Executor health. Level sanity check at 18:00; no bar for 2 minutes means pull pendings and manage only; any reject or fill mismatch halts new entries; daily reconcile against the broker.
G10
Instrument dials. Gold trades only with its volatility dial on; NQ runs undialled.
§2 · §6 · §32

The honesty stack, and what to attack

Haircuts already taken

  • Fills must earn it. A limit only fills if price trades a full tick through the level. Touch fills would flatter the raw edge by roughly 40%.
  • Costs in, per contract. 0.5pt per round trip, subtracted in R against each trade's own stop.
  • Ambiguity counts as a loss. When a bar could have hit stop and target, it is scored a loss. Fill-bar stop-outs are taken.
  • No lookahead. Prior-day levels, closed candles, forward-only fills and exits.
  • Levels are computed, not read off a chart — and re-running with the measured level error injected keeps 86–89% of the edge.

Known idealisations

  • No queue or latency model beyond the through-the-level fill rule. This is the biggest remaining gap.
  • The cross-book rail pass is post-hoc chronological. An exact joint simulation is executor-stage work.
  • No cost shocks, no missed sessions — which is part of why a simulated Sharpe should not be read as a live forecast.
  • ~80 trades a day is automation only. None of this is hand-tradeable, and it was never designed to be.
  • Gold sits outside this stream — its own constants, its own volatility dial, certified separately.
